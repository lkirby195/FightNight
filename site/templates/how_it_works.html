How it works

One rating system, two rules, no exceptions

The ratings

Every UFC fight since 1994 runs through the same rating system, one fight at a time. A new fighter starts at 1500. Around 1800 is a contender; 2000 and up is champion territory. Beating a strong opponent moves you up more than beating a weak one. Each fighter also carries a margin of error: wide for a newcomer, narrower with every fight, wider again after a long layoff. The rankings sort by rating minus that margin, so a fighter has to keep earning the spot.

The engine is a Glicko-2 variant (spec in the repo). A debutant is 1500 with an RD of 350; RD shrinks with results and inflates with time away (volatility tau {{ tau }}). The rankings list rating minus RD for fighters with a bout in the last 18 months. Engine parameters were frozen on {{ ratings_freeze }} and are not tuned on anything since.

Finishes count more than decisions

A knockout or a submission is a clear answer. A unanimous decision is a fairly clear answer. A split decision tells us almost nothing about who's better, and the ratings treat it that way: it barely moves either fighter.

Result weights: a finish scores 1.00 for the winner, a unanimous or majority decision {{ s_udec }}, a split decision {{ s_sdec }} (so the loser of a split keeps {{ (1 - s_sdec) | round(2) }}). The split-decision weight was fitted on fights through 2019 and checked on 2020 onward; the data pushed it from the 0.75 we started with down to {{ s_sdec }}, close to a coin flip. A disqualification scores like a split. A heavy favourite who wins a split decision can lose rating on the night, which is the intended signal.

How a pick becomes a bet

Before each card we combine the ratings with what each fighter has done in the cage (pace, finishes, takedowns, age, layoffs, reach) and put a number on every fight: our probability that each side wins. Our pick is the side with the higher number. We post it before the fight and never change it afterwards.

Most weeks the odds and our numbers roughly agree, and we do nothing. We bet only when we disagree with the odds by a lot, in one of two ways. The bar for each was fixed on {{ rule_freeze }}:

  • The underdog rule. The odds make a fighter the underdog, but our number has that fighter winning at least {{ (flip * 100) | round | int }}% of the time.
  • The price rule. We agree with the favourite, but the odds pay at least {{ gap }} points more than our number says they should.

How much we bet was fixed the next day, {{ policy_freeze }}: one unit (${{ 100 }}) when we know the fighter we're backing well, sliding to half a unit as the track record thins. We skip long shots (anything +{{ skip_line | int }} or longer, more than {{ (skip_line / 100) | round(1) }}-to-1 against) and fights where we don't know either fighter well enough to trust the number. A fighter with no UFC bout on record gets no pick at all.

Signal rules: FLIP when the model's pick is the market underdog at the opening line with P(pick) ≥ {{ flip }}; GAP when the pick is the market favourite and its fair line sits ≥ {{ gap }} American points past the open; a pick'em open has no favourite, so the GAP test applies. Bouts whose opening pair sums outside [1.00, 1.12] implied probability are skipped. The open is the validated open: a first tick corrected within the hour by more than 40 points is replaced by the correction, and a signal that fires only against the bad tick is logged VOID, never bet. Placement policy v1: stake 1u up to a backed-side RD of {{ ramp[0] }}, falling linearly to 0.5u at RD {{ ramp[1] }} and floored there; no bet when the backed side is longer than +{{ skip_line | int }} or when both fighters' RD exceeds {{ unknown_rd }}. Every signal is still logged at flat 1u so the rules can be judged apart from the sizing.

How the record is kept

The live record starts on Sep 8, 2026. That is the day the betting rules froze and the day we began committing every pick with a timestamp before the fight, together with the price that was on the board. Nothing is edited after. Results are graded at that posted price, not at the opening line and not at the closing line. Every page is generated from the ledger and committed with it, so any change after the fact would show in the history.

Everything before that date is backtest: the 2012–2025 run of these same rules, and the 2026 picks before we published. The backtest is the evidence for the system; the live record is the test of it. Backtest picks are shown, never counted. Download the ledger.

Since {{ "September 2026" }} every posted line is captured in an append-only file committed with the card (the "placeable" line); live bets settle there, earlier live bets settle at the opening line. CLV (closing-line value) is the picked side's vig-free closing probability minus its opening probability, in points; the close is the last pre-fight two-sided book, in-play ticks excluded.
